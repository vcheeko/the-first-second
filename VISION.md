# THE FIRST SECOND — Visual and Experience Vision

## Target experience

THE FIRST SECOND is intended as a **high-end 3D simulation game with cinematic presentation and strong visual fidelity**.

The current browser prototype is **not** the intended final visual style, platform, rendering quality, or engine direction. It exists only to validate core mechanics cheaply and quickly before expensive 3D production begins.

## Visual ambition

The long-term target is a world that can make different layers of reality feel physically convincing and emotionally distinct.

Desired qualities include:

- realistic or near-realistic 3D environments;
- cinematic lighting and atmosphere;
- high-detail materials and surfaces;
- strong scale, depth and environmental presence;
- convincing transitions between physical, metaphase, light/information and other states of existence;
- visually dramatic simulation boundaries and anomalies;
- seamless-feeling transitions between different realities where technically feasible;
- a premium presentation standard rather than a stylized browser-game look.

## Why the early prototype is simple

Before committing to expensive 3D assets, animation, rendering pipelines or a final game engine, the project first tests whether its core ideas actually work as gameplay.

The browser prototype therefore answers questions such as:

- Do seconds feel different from a normal health bar?
- Does lower stability create curiosity as well as danger?
- Can a player understand that different states of existence are becoming possible?
- Does reaching a simulation boundary feel meaningful?

If those systems work, they can then be rebuilt in a dedicated 3D production environment.

## 3D production gate

A serious 3D vertical slice should begin only after the core seconds, identity, memory and simulation-transition rules are coherent enough that expensive art production will not be repeatedly discarded.

The future engine decision should be based on the needs of the vertical slice, including:

- real-time high-fidelity rendering;
- world scale;
- lighting;
- physics;
- animation;
- visual effects;
- simulation transitions;
- target hardware;
- development resources and team skills.

No current browser implementation should be treated as a commitment to the final engine or architecture.

## Canonical direction

**Mechanics prototype now. High-end cinematic 3D game later.**
